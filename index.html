Family nutrition guide

Small Meals,
More Energy

For families of children ages 1 to 5 who need help gaining weight

If your child’s care team has suggested extra calories, start with a food your child already likes. A small add-in can give them more energy even if they eat about the same amount.

Add to foods your child already eats

Pick one idea that fits what your family has on hand. Try it at the next meal or snack.

Oatmeal + plain full-fat yogurt

Stir the yogurt into warm oatmeal. It adds calories and protein.

Mashed beans + avocado

Mash ripe avocado into the beans for extra calories.

Soft pasta + olive oil

Toss warm pasta with a little olive oil before serving.

Start with what works for your child. Choose foods that fit their allergies and serve them in a form your child can chew and swallow safely.

Make time for meals and snacks

Offer meals and snacks at regular times. Start with a small serving and let your child decide how much to eat. You can offer more if they’re still hungry.

  • Breakfast
  • Morning snack
  • Lunch
  • Afternoon snack
  • Dinner

If your child often fills up on juice or sweet drinks, offer water between meals.

Make a plan for follow-up

Ask your child’s doctor or dietitian to help you choose what to try and when to check growth again.

  • What can I add to a food my child likes?
  • When should we check their growth again?
  • Would a nutrition drink help? If so, when should I offer it?

Tell the care team if your child often coughs or has trouble swallowing while eating.
